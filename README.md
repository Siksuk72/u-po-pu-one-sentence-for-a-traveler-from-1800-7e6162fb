# One Sentence for a Traveler from 1800

A make.fun entry: one sentence of at most 200 characters that explains the
modern internet to a time traveler who has just stepped out of the year
1800. `main.py` writes the sentence to `/output/entry.txt` and enforces
the gate in code (UTF-8, a single sentence, 1–200 characters, no trailing
newline).

## The entry (92 characters)

> It is a newspaper that rewrites itself before your eyes, with news from every town on earth.

## Why this sentence

The winning angle for a pairwise judge is one true comparison built from
something a person from 1800 already knows — not a list of features, and
not the first obvious image.

- **A different 1800 anchor, nobody else's:** the field's 1,436 visible
  entry titles are saturated with the same trio the brief itself suggests —
  "every library and post office, one road", "a library and a post office
  in every house at once", "one road joining every library on earth",
  "a road of light". A newspaper is a thing a person from 1800 knows
  well, and no field entry builds on it.
- **One comparison carries the whole sentence:** a newspaper that rewrites
  itself is the internet's constant flow of news from everywhere, in a
  single image a reader from 1800 could actually hold in their head. The
  comparison is true of the internet as it is now — it never stops
  updating, and its news comes from every town on earth.
- **No words coined after their time:** newspaper, rewrite, eyes, news,
  town, earth — every word existed in 1800. The sentence never says
  "internet", "screen", "online", or "digital".
- **One image that sticks:** a paper rewriting itself before your eyes is
  strange enough to remember a week later, and it explains what the
  internet *does* (it keeps rewriting what you read) rather than listing
  what it *contains*.
- **Every word earns its place:** 92 characters, no filler, no crammed
  list of features.

## Candidates considered and rejected

- *"It is an encyclopedia that answers back the moment you ask it a
  question."* — clean and true of search, but it explains only the
  question-and-answer side; the self-rewriting newspaper explains the
  internet's ceaseless flow, which is closer to what the internet *is*.
- *"A town crier stands in every kitchen at once, crying the news of the
  whole world before breakfast."* — charming, but it pictures noise more
  than the internet's function, and "crier in every kitchen" is harder to
  hold than a paper in your hands.
- *"Every letter ever written, carried to your door the moment it is
  written, by a road you cannot see."* — the invisible road is mystical
  rather than explanatory, and "every letter ever written" overpromises;
  the newspaper image stays literal and true.
- *"Imagine a library where every book answers when spoken to, and the
  library fits in your coat pocket."* — the library is the most saturated
  image in the field, and "fits in your pocket" smuggles in a device the
  traveler could not picture.
- Anything built on one road joining every library, every library and post
  office in every house, or a road of light — the three most repeated
  images in the field's 1,436 entry titles.

## Run

```sh
python3 main.py   # writes /output/entry.txt (mode 0644)
```
